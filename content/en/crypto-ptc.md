Crypto PTC works like normal [paid to click](/paid-to-click/): you open an ad, wait out a timer, and get a small reward. The difference is that the reward is in cryptocurrency, and that changes a few practical things.

## Why crypto PTC sites pay through FaucetPay

PTC rewards are tiny. Sending 0.004 DOGE directly to your wallet would cost more in network fees than it is worth on many coins. So most crypto PTC sites, including the ones advertised as FaucetPay PTC, pay into a microwallet. FaucetPay is the most common one: rewards from many sites build up in one balance, and you withdraw when the total makes sense.

## Bitcoin PTC sites

People still search for bitcoin PTC sites, and a few exist. They tend to pay in satoshis, and you will usually withdraw to FaucetPay or convert inside it. If you specifically want bitcoin, check whether the site pays BTC directly or pays another coin and lets you swap.

## Crypto PTC versus classic PTC

| | Classic PTC | Crypto PTC |
|---|---|---|
| Paid in | USD, points, gift cards | DOGE, LTC, TRX, BTC and others |
| Minimum withdrawal | Often several dollars | Can be a few cents' worth |
| Payment route | PayPal, bank, gift card | Usually FaucetPay |
| Price risk | None | Your earnings change with the coin price |

## What to check before you start

- **The coin.** Earnings in a coin you do not want mean extra swaps and fees.
- **The minimum withdrawal.** Lower is better, especially for a first test.
- **Who the advertisers are.** A healthy site has ads for real products, not only for other crypto earning sites.
- **Account rules.** Most sites, SurfFlux included, ban multiple accounts and link accounts through FaucetPay identities and device signals. Breaking those rules usually means losing the balance.
