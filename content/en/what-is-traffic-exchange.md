A traffic exchange is a site where members view each other's websites. You visit other people's pages, earn credits for each visit, and spend those credits to have your own page shown to other members. Nobody pays cash unless they want to skip the earning part and buy credits directly.

The idea is old. Exchanges were already common in the early 2000s, when small site owners had few other ways to get any visitors at all.

## The basic loop

1. You add your website to the exchange.
2. You view other members' sites. Each view usually has a timer, somewhere between a few seconds and a minute.
3. Each completed view earns credits.
4. Your credits are spent showing your site to other members.

The **ratio** tells you how fair the deal is. On a 1:1 exchange, one view you make buys one view of your site. Many exchanges use worse ratios for free members and better ones for paid plans.

## Manual surfing versus autosurf

On a **manual** exchange you click to the next site yourself, and often answer a simple check to prove you are there. On an **autosurf** exchange, a program rotates sites without anyone touching it. Autosurf is easier, so it is more popular, but the visits are mostly unattended.

Some newer exchanges sit in between. SurfFlux, a [traffic exchange that pays in DOGE](https://surfflux.com/), for example, offers PTC ads where you open one page at a time and pass a check at the end, and a browser extension that opens advertiser pages in real tabs.

## Where cryptocurrency comes in

Classic exchanges pay in credits only. Some newer ones let you choose: keep your rewards as advertising credit, or withdraw them as cryptocurrency. On SurfFlux, validated activity adds DOGE to your balance, which you can either spend on your own campaigns or withdraw through FaucetPay.

## What traffic exchange visits are good for

- Checking that a new page loads and tracks correctly under real browser visits.
- Getting a first trickle of impressions for a banner, a video or a splash page.
- Learning how timers, bounce rate and analytics behave.

## What they are not good for

- **Sales.** Most visitors are there to earn, not to buy.
- **Pages with AdSense.** Google does not allow exchange traffic on AdSense pages and bans accounts that use it.
- **SEO.** Short, low-engagement visits will not improve your search rankings.
