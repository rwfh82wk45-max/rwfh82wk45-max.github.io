FaucetPay faucets and earning sites: how to pick the ones worth your time

By the SurfFlux team · Updated 2026-10-30

A FaucetPay faucet is any site that pays your rewards into a FaucetPay account instead of straight to a blockchain address. The amounts are tiny, often a fraction of a cent per claim, so sending each one on-chain would cost more in network fees than the reward itself. FaucetPay collects them in one balance and lets you withdraw once the total is worth it.

That is the whole appeal. It is also why the list of "best FaucetPay sites" changes every few months: faucets come and go, cut their rates, or stop paying, while FaucetPay itself stays put.

What actually pays into FaucetPay

"Faucet" has become a loose word. Sites that pay into FaucetPay fall into a few groups:

  • Classic faucets. Solve a captcha, claim a small amount, wait for a timer. Low effort, low pay, lots of ads on the page.
  • PTC (paid to click) sites. You open an advertiser's page and keep it open for 10 to 60 seconds or so. Pay per view is usually higher than a faucet claim. Our guide to crypto PTC sites goes into detail.
  • Traffic exchanges and surf sites. A browser extension opens advertiser pages in tabs while you do something else. SurfFlux works this way and pays in DOGE.
  • Offerwalls and shortlinks. Higher amounts per task, more friction, and more variety in quality.
  • "Mining" sites. Most so-called FaucetPay mining sites do not mine anything. They show a hashrate counter that grows as you watch ads or buy upgrades. Treat any site that asks you to pay for a faster "miner" with suspicion.

Which faucets are best quality? A five-minute checklist

There is no permanent top ten, so here is what we check before spending time on a site.

  1. Withdrawal threshold versus pay rate. Work out how many claims or ads it takes to reach the minimum. If it is hundreds of hours, the site is not really paying you.
  2. Instant or manual payouts. Sites that pay to FaucetPay automatically and quickly are easier to trust than ones that "process payments weekly".
  3. Recent payment proof from other users. Look for forum threads or reviews dated in the last month, not a screenshot from 2023.
  4. What the ads look like. Aggressive redirects, fake download buttons and notification prompts are a sign the owner earns more from your clicks than from honest advertising.
  5. No deposit required to withdraw. A faucet that wants money from you before you can cash out is not a faucet.

Where to find FaucetPay sites

FaucetPay keeps a directory of sites connected to it, which is the safest starting point because the site owners have to use a FaucetPay account to pay you. Beyond that, faucet list sites and forum threads are useful, but check the date on everything.
